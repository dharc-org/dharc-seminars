Step 2 · An inverted index: word → chunks

index = {token: set of chunk ids}

Query {whom, virgin, mary, appear, 1858} → candidates {12, 4871, 18203}. Chunk 9034 is never scored. On 25,588 chunks that is what keeps search fast.

Step 3 · Score: which words count, and how much?

Weight words by

To whom did the Virgin Mary appear in 1858 in Lourdes, France?

a word that appears in of 25,000 chunks → idf

idf(t) = log(1 + N / df(t)), N ≈ 25,000. Logarithmic x axis.

BM25: IDF plus two refinements

score = Σ idf(t) × f(t,c) × (k1 + 1) / ( f(t,c) + k1 × (1 − b + b × len(c) / avg_len) )

Term frequency, with saturation

k1 1.5
1×2×3×5×10×∞

Contribution of a word that occurs n times, relative to once.

Length normalization

b 0.75

One match, k1 = 1.5, avg_len = 100 words. With b = 0 length is ignored.

Rank 1 is read best

Models use the top (and the end) of a long context more reliably than the middle: "lost in the middle" (Liu et al., 2023; exercise 3).

That is why MRR matters

recall@5 says the evidence arrived. MRR says it arrived where the model looks.

What to take away

  1. Measure the stages separately. "57% correct" is hard to act on. "Retrieval found the evidence for 28 of 30, the model answered 17 of those 28" points to two different fixes.
  2. Build a baseline first. It shows how much the language model really adds.
  3. Audit the output. A prompt is a request. The gap between has_citation and valid_citations shows why checks must be mechanical.
  4. Keep infrastructure failures out of your results. An empty response is a bug to fix, not a wrong answer.
  5. Change one thing at a time. Freezing the retriever is what let us blame every difference on the answering step.
  6. Sample fairly, and put an error bar on it. Shuffle before slicing; on 30 questions, 0.57 means "somewhere between 0.37 and 0.73".

Eleven exercises, one change each

For after the workshop. Each changes a single component, reuses what you built, and has a solution and readings. Write your prediction first.

Predict first, then open the solution

1 · Chunk size

50/10, 100/20, 200/40. What happens to coverage, and to recall@5?

click for the hint ↻

Coverage rises with size; recall may not. Big chunks match many queries weakly. Coverage is a ceiling on recall.

2 · BM25's b

b = 0, 0.75, 1. Chunks are at most 100 words. Does b matter here?

click for the hint ↻

Barely: only the short tail chunks differ. b was designed for collections mixing tweets and books.

3 · Context budget

k = 1, 5, 10. More evidence, or more distractors?

click for the hint ↻

k alone misleads: with 500 words, k = 10 delivers about five chunks. Watch exact_match and latency together.

4 · Abstention threshold

Abstain below a top-score cutoff. What do you gain and lose?

click for the hint ↻

The distributions overlap: report fabrications prevented and answers lost. The cutoff belongs to this corpus.

5 · Model comparison

Pull another model and re-run. What must you report with the score?

click for the hint ↻

Model, retriever, k, budget, latency. Compare valid_citations as carefully as exact_match. Run twice: the first call loads the model.

6 · Embeddings

Dense vectors vs BM25, then fuse them. Which wins on SQuAD?

click for the hint ↻

Each wins on some questions. SQuAD favours words; paraphrase and old spelling favour embeddings. Fusion often beats both.

7 · Reranking

20 candidates, re-sorted by the model. Worth the calls?

click for the hint ↻

Count calls first: questions × pool. If it brings little on SQuAD, that's a valid finding.

8 · Injection defence

Poison 10 questions. Does repeating the rules after the context help?

click for the hint ↻

Count before and after. One wording, one position: attackers iterate, so should your test. valid_citations is the hard defence.

9 · Is the gap real?

Two runs, same questions. Paired bootstrap of the difference.

click for the hint ↻

If the interval of the difference excludes 0, it's unlikely to be luck. Pairing is sharper than two separate intervals.

10 · LLM as judge

Ask a model whether the answer means the same as the gold.

click for the hint ↻

It catches paraphrases EM misses (fifth / 5th). A judge errs too: validate it on hand-labelled answers.

11 · Query rewriting

Search with the question plus a passage the model imagines (HyDE).

click for the hint ↻

Helps word matching on SQuAD, but the model knows Wikipedia: test it on your own archive.
